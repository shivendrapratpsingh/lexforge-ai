What this app is, in one paragraph

A lawyer who goes to court spends most of their working life not arguing, but typing. Formal documents in formats the court is strict about. Hunting through old judgments for one that supports their argument. Keeping track of dozens of hearing dates. LexForge does the typing and the hunting. The lawyer still decides what to argue — the machine writes the draft and finds the judgments, which turns hours into minutes.

Live at lexforge-ai.vercel.app. Free plan gives 10 documents a month; Pro removes the cap and unlocks case-law search and live case tracking.

First

Setting up, once

Two minutes. The only part that matters later is step 3.

Create your account

  1. Go to lexforge-ai.vercel.app and tap Create one free.
  2. Enter your name, email, and a password of at least 8 characters.
  3. Answer the security question — “What is the nickname you have kept for your best friend?” This is how you get back in if you forget your password. There is no reset email. Capitals and spacing don’t matter, so “Bunty”, “bunty” and “ BUNTY ” all work. Pick something you’ll remember in two years and that isn’t on your Instagram.
  4. Tap Create Account. You land straight on the dashboard.
You’re in. The dashboard is the page you’ll see every time you sign in.

Put it on your phone’s home screen

Optional, but it then behaves like a normal app and can send you a morning reminder.

Worth knowing: no web app on Android or iPhone can put a widget on your home screen — only the app icon. The widgets live inside the app, and the daily 8 AM notification is what actually reaches your phone.
The main job

Writing a legal document

This is what most people open the app for. Twenty kinds of document, four different ways to start.

The basic run-through

Example used here: a to a tenant who won’t leave.

Legal notice — a formal warning letter you send before suing someone. It says: here is what you did, here is what I want, do it by this date or I go to court. For many disputes you are legally required to send one first, and it often ends the matter without a case at all.
  1. From the sidebar, tap New Document.
  2. Pick the document type — here, 📋 Legal Notice.
  3. Choose how you want to enter the facts. Four options, explained below. Most people start with Fill Form.
  4. Fill in the facts. For a legal notice that’s nine fields: who is sending, who is receiving, both addresses, the complaint, what you’re demanding, and the deadline. Leave a field blank if you don’t know it. The app writes [DATE — TO BE FILLED] rather than inventing something.
  5. Pick the court, and the language if not English.
  6. Tap Generate. It takes roughly thirty seconds.
  7. Read what comes back, edit anything you want, then download or copy it.
What you get: a complete document in the format that court expects — correct heading, numbered paragraphs, the right closing, a proper .
Verification clause — the sworn bit at the end where you confirm the contents are true. Courts take it seriously: a document without one, or with the wording wrong, can be thrown out before a judge reads a word of it.

The four ways to enter your facts

Same document, four starting points. Use whichever suits how you actually work.

  1. Fill Form — a form with exactly the fields that document needs.Best when you have the details in front of you. Nothing gets forgotten.
  2. Smart Q&A — it asks one question at a time.Best on a phone, or when you’ve never made this kind of document before.
  3. Paste Document — paste something that already exists; it reads it and pulls the details out.Best for replying to or amending something.
  4. Upload Folder / File — upload the client’s papers and it extracts what it needs.The one people react to. Clients arrive with a bundle of papers, not neat facts.

Writing in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu or Urdu

District courts across much of India don’t work in English.

  1. On the New Document screen, find the language selector before you generate.
  2. Pick English, Hindi, Urdu, Tamil, Telugu, or English + Hindi side by side.
  3. Generate as normal.
It writes in that language — it does not write English and translate. Legal Hindi isn’t conversational Hindi; it has its own formal vocabulary that courts expect, and the app carries a dictionary of the proper terms.
The best feature

Finding judgments that support your case

Describe your situation in ordinary words; get back the past judgments that bear on it.

Find cases like mine

It’s the first thing on the dashboard when you sign in.

  1. On the dashboard, find the Find cases like mine panel and tap Start.
  2. In “What is the matter about?”, describe it plainly. Genuinely plainly — full sentences, the way you’d tell a friend. Example: “My client issued a cheque for ₹4,00,000 which bounced for insufficient funds. The other side now says the signature was forged.”
  3. Optionally add what you’re asking the court for, the matter type, and any law you already have in mind.
  4. Tap Find relevant cases.
What you get, in this order: the statutes that apply, then judgments grouped by court — Supreme Court first, then High Courts, then tribunals. That order isn’t decoration: it’s ranked by how much each one binds the judge hearing your case.

Above the results it shows the search it actually ran. For the cheque example that was:

Section 138 Negotiable Instruments Act dishonour of cheque for insufficient funds forged signature defence

That’s shown deliberately — so you can see whether it understood your matter before you trust anything below it. Names, dates and amounts are stripped out first, because they narrow a case-law search to nothing.

The important safeguard. The AI is never allowed to name a judgment. It only writes the search. Every case you see came out of a real database of 30 million judgments, with its own citation and a link to the full text — so you can open it and read the original. Lawyers abroad have been punished for filing invented case citations; this is built specifically so that can’t happen.
Tracking

Looking up a live case

Type a case number, see everything the court’s own system knows.

Look up a case by CNR

  1. Sidebar → Case Law & Status.
  2. Tap the 🔎 Case by CNR tab.
  3. Type the , e.g. DLHC010001232024, then tap Look up case.
CNR number — the unique 16-character code every court case in India has. Think of it as the case’s Aadhaar number. It’s printed on every order and every daily hearing list.
What you get: the parties, what stage it’s reached, the next hearing date, and the orders passed. From then on the case is tracked — every morning at 8 the app re-checks it, so the next date on your dashboard is always current without you looking it up.

Searching judgments directly, and new laws

  1. Same page, ⚖️ Judgments tab — search case law by words or by court.
  2. 📜 New Acts tab — what the app found this morning.Every day at 8 AM it checks India Code, the Government’s own official list of every law, for Acts newly in force.
Why that matters: Indian law changes constantly. In 2024 the whole criminal code was replaced — every section number a lawyer had memorised for twenty years changed. Citing a cancelled section can sink your case. This keeps the library current on its own.
Asking questions

Legal research

Ask a legal question in plain language and get an answer with the law behind it.

Analyse a legal issue

  1. Sidebar → Legal Research.
  2. In the AI Legal Analysis box, describe your question.Example: “A tenant is refusing to vacate after the lease expired. What remedies are available?”
  3. Tap 🤖 Analyze with AI.
What you get: four headings — Legal Analysis, Applicable Law, Supporting Judgments, Recommended Course of Action — and beneath them a list of Verified sources, each linking to the original judgment.
Read this bit. If it says “No verified case law”, that means no judgments were found — and the analysis will then cite none at all, rather than making some up. That’s deliberate. An answer with no cases is far safer than an answer with invented ones.

Searching the case-law database

  1. Same page, left side, Case Law Database.
  2. Type what you’re looking for.
  3. Tap 🔎 Search the full case-law index for the real 30-million-judgment search.Without tapping it you’re only seeing 8 famous landmark cases kept offline as a quick reference — useful, but not a database.

Running a practice

Clients, dates and the morning brief

Most Indian lawyers run everything from a paper diary. This is the diary that reminds you back.

Add a client

  1. Sidebar → Clients → Add Client.
  2. Fill in what you have — name, contact, address, ID. Blank fields are fine.
  3. Open the client to attach documents, log payments, and add notes.
Everything links up: that client’s documents, hearing dates and payments all sit together.

Add a hearing date

  1. Sidebar → Court Dates → add a date.
  2. Give it a title, the date, and a type: hearing, filing, compliance or deadline.
  3. Link it to a client or a document if you want them connected.
Anything overdue appears as a red Action Required banner at the top of your dashboard until you deal with it.

Turn on the 8 AM brief

  1. On the dashboard, find the Morning notification card.
  2. Choose your topics: court dates, draft quota, study prompt.
  3. Tap Turn on daily brief and allow notifications.
  4. Use Send a test to check it works.
iPhone only: you must add the app to your Home Screen first and open it from there. Apple doesn’t deliver notifications to a Safari tab. That’s their restriction, not a bug in the app.
One notification a day, deliberately — your next hearing, drafts waiting for your signature, a study prompt, and a line worth reading. There are 542 of those lines, so it takes over a year to repeat.

Straight talk

What it doesn’t do

Worth knowing before you rely on it, and before you recommend it to anyone.

It writes a strong first draft, not a finished filing

Simple documents — notices, RTI, affidavits, rent agreements — come out close to final: read them, fill the blanks, sign. Complicated ones — writ petitions, PILs, the grounds in a bail application — come out as a solid skeleton that a lawyer must strengthen. Nothing should be filed unread.

It can’tWhy that matters
Have a strategyIt doesn’t know this judge dislikes that argument. That’s why a lawyer reads it.
Check whether facts are trueIf the client lied or you typed a wrong date, it faithfully reproduces the error.
Do property title checksEspecially for a sale deed. Verifying ownership and boundaries is human work.
Handle stamp duty and registrationThese vary by state. Getting them wrong can make a document unenforceable.
The honest summary: it does the four hours of typing and searching. It doesn’t do the twenty minutes of thinking — and it shouldn’t. Your name goes on the document.
